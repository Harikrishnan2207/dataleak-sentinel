Generated by `python -m sentinel.evaluation_heldout`; deterministic and synthetic. **Read this next to `EVALUATION.md`.** The main report was re-run after the defaults were changed until every attack was caught, so its headline numbers are in-sample. This report shows what the tuning changed, how the result holds up on reshaped scenarios, and where the thresholds actually sit.

## D. A side effect of the 12-hour cumulative rule: follow-on alerts

One legitimate 420-row export, then tiny lookups over the next hours (current settings):

| Time | Rows | Level | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| 09:00 | 420 | High | 76 |
| 10:42 | 3 | Medium | 36 |
| 12:24 | 3 | Medium | 36 |
| 14:06 | 3 | Medium | 36 |
| 15:48 | 3 | Medium | 36 |
| 17:30 | 3 | Medium | 36 |
| 19:12 | 3 | Medium | 36 |
| 20:54 | 3 | Medium | 36 |

The export itself alerts, and so does each of the next 7 three-row lookups, because the running total stays above the limit for 12 hours. One incident becomes many alerts.

## What this shows

- **In-sample vs reshaped:** the current settings detect 100% of attack episodes on the data they were tuned on, but 84% on reshaped scenarios, a gap of 16 points. The previous settings detect 71% and 68%. Treat the in-sample figure as an upper bound, not as accuracy.
- **Weakness:** `low_volume_spike` is detected in 100% of original episodes but only 88% of reshaped ones.
- **Weakness:** `sub_threshold_drip` is detected in 100% of original episodes but only 19% of reshaped ones.
- **Spike sweep:** an alert fires in at least half the trials from 12 rows with the current settings, never within the tested range with the previous ones (best 48%).
- **Drip sweep:** detection reaches half the trials at about 16 rows per query (about 480 rows in total, against the 400-row cumulative limit). At smaller sizes, 51% to 98% of trials were never alerted at all. Even above the limit the alert comes late (see the share-read column).
- **Cost:** a legitimate busy working day is flagged 32% of the time with the previous settings and 51% with the current ones.
- **Alert fatigue:** after one large access every later access for 12 hours is also flagged (7 follow-on alerts in the example above).

## Limits of this report

- I wrote the reshaped scenarios and sweeps after the current settings were known, by the same author as the detector. The sweeps are the fairer view because they show the whole range, but the shapes (30 queries, 20 to 45 minutes apart, light users) are still my assumptions.
- Real attackers adapt. An attacker who knows a fixed limit stays under it, and the sweeps show where that limit sits.
- Nothing here measures performance on real users or real attacks.
- Reproduce with `python -m sentinel.evaluation_heldout` (under a minute). Changing the code, the seeds or the settings changes the numbers.
